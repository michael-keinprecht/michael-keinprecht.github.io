## Working Papers

[**Third party loss aversion reduces spectator distribution:**](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5962280) In a preregistered online experiment with a within-subjects design, I show that redistribution decisions by third parties are affected by loss aversion (therefore third-party loss aversion). Overall, spectators are twice as likely (i.e., 7 percentage points more likely) to choose a status quo over an alternative if the alternative results in a loss for one person, even if the alternative is favored by inequality aversion, maximin preferences and efficiency concerns. The effect is larger when the loss is higher compared to the gain and when the spectator's individual loss aversion is higher.

## Ongoing Projects

**Shifting Parental Stereotypes: Investment Responses to Information about STEM [with Elisabeth Gsottbauer and Simone Haeckl]:** We tested two intervention videos in an information experiment. We find that the video focussing on STEM careers increases parents' willingness to invest, particularly in budget allocation tasks, while effects on incentivized outcomes are smaller. The video debunking gender stereotypes shows little impact, and neither treatment shifts career aspirations. Results suggest that highlighting what modern STEM jobs are is actually more effective than challenging beliefs about innate ability when aiming to influence parent-driven investments.

**Distributing Opportunities vs Outcomes: Experimental Evidence on Wage-Setting:** In a simple dictator game with a work stage, dictators are asked to set the wages either before or after the work stage. This conceptualizes in a simple way the difference between equality of opportunity and equality of outcome. I find no significant treatment differences between before and after decisions. These surprising null results suggest that in this specific setting, people stick with their homegrown conception of fairness, and are not influenced by the timing of the wage decision and therefore the lack of information on relative merit.

**What the Public Means by Social Mobility: Preferences over Dimensions and Indicators [with Moritz Hörl]:** This project investigates how the general public conceptualizes and values social mobility across four dimensions (income, education, occupation, and wealth) and three indicators (Absolute Mobility, Intergenerational Elasticity, and Conditional Expected Rank). Preliminary results suggest that Income > Wealth > Education > Occupation and CER25 ≈ AM ≫ IGE.
